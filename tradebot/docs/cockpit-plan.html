Draken Cockpit Plan

How the trading bot should look, how you control it, and where it should trade. The plan draws on two research passes: one on exchanges and fees, one on open-source bots and competitors. One finding shapes everything else: at €90, fees are the opponent to beat.

Finding 1

Fees decide whether any strategy can win

Kraken raised its lowest-tier fees on 9 July 2026 to 0.40 % maker and 0.80 % taker (I confirmed this on Kraken's own fee page). Each trade pays twice, once to buy and once to sell, so every trade has to earn at least this much just to break even:

1.60 %Kraken, market orders both ways
0.50 %Bitvavo, market orders both ways
0.40 %OKX EU, market orders both ways
The front end

The cockpit: four charts that teach

Each panel answers one question a beginner should ask of any trading bot. The data below is invented to show the design. The real cockpit reads live numbers from the bot.

Beyond these four, the cockpit also has a per-trade chart showing the candles, the indicator values, and the exact reason the bot entered and exited. There is also a paper-vs-backtest band: if live paper results fall outside the range the backtest predicted, the cockpit flags it.

Draken cockpit

  • The teaching dashboard above
  • One self-contained HTML file, same style as the other Draken modules

No interface can turn on real money. Going live requires you to edit the config file by hand and to add exchange API keys that can trade but never withdraw. This protects against a leaked or hijacked key, the way 3Commas users lost $14.8M in 2022.

Bitvavo offers ready-made candle history, so testing there would skip the slow Kraken download entirely. Its fees are about a third of Kraken's. One gap: Bitvavo cannot hold a stop-loss order on its side, so Freqtrade has to watch the stop-loss itself.

Swedish tax. Every sale or swap is a taxable disposal. You declare them on K4 section D using the average-cost method (genomsnittsmetoden). Gains are taxed at 30 %, and only 70 % of losses are deductible. A bot trading daily creates hundreds of disposals a year. Divly produces a K4 directly. Koinly's plans that cover that many transactions cost more than the whole €90 stake.

Finding 3

What the open-source world teaches

What we borrow

  1. Automatic bias checks. Freqtrade's lookahead-analysis and recursive-analysis run on every strategy change.
  2. Fixed test periods covering a bull, a bear and a sideways market, as NostalgiaForInfinity does in its test pipeline.
  3. Walk-forward testing. Tune only on in-sample data and never touch the last 3 months.
  4. Parameter heatmaps (vectorbt). Pick a stable region rather than the single best point.
  5. Limit orders to pay the maker fee, which halves the cost. We also borrow Hummingbot's separation of trading signals from order handling.

Traps we avoid

  1. Ignoring fees. This alone kills most 5- and 15-minute strategies.
  2. Over-tuning. In a study of 888 Quantopian strategies, backtest performance barely predicted live results (R² < 0.025).
  3. Leaderboards. They show backtests only, with no verified live records. One leaderboard site, strat.ninja, has already disappeared.
  4. Survivorship. Pick pairs from what existed at the start of the test period, not today's winners.
  5. API keys that allow withdrawals. Ours will only ever be trade-only.

The research also shows which edges actually survive fees. Slow trend-following on hourly or daily candles holds up best, mainly because it avoids big crashes. Fast mean reversion is real but tiny: about 0.013 % per trade against our 1.6 % cost. Market making and funding-rate carry need sizes or products we don't have.
